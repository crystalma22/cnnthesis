## 📝 HOW TO EXPLAIN EACH

### In Text (Table References):

**Example for Table 3:**
> "Table 3 reports equal-weighted portfolio performance. Returns increase monotonically from -28.08% (lowest-predicted stocks) to +42.66% (highest-predicted), yielding an H-L spread of 70.74% with Sharpe ratio of 5.60. Formal monotonicity tests confirm perfect rank correlation (Spearman ρ=1.00, p<0.001)."

**Example for Table 5:**
> "Table 5 presents FOMC event study results. Equal-weighted spreads are statistically significant on announcement days (0.21%, t=2.95, p<0.01) and in the intermediate window (0.35%, t=2.24, p=0.03). These effects are robust to outlier treatment: means closely track medians (0.208% vs 0.205% for announcement day) and dropping extreme events changes estimates by less than 2-12%."

**Example for Table 6:**
> "Table 6 reports event-level comparison using matched sampling. FOMC days exhibit uniformly lower CNN predictability than matched non-FOMC days across all horizons (differences -0.49% to -0.87%, t-statistics -4.17 to -11.90, all p<0.001). This pattern supports the attention-based efficiency hypothesis: when markets focus on systematic macro shocks, cross-sectional patterns get compressed."

### In Text (Figure References):

**Example for Figure 4:**
> "Figure 4 visualizes FOMC event study results. The announcement-day effect (0.21%) and intermediate effect (0.35%) are both statistically significant for equal-weighted portfolios, while value-weighted effects are small and generally insignificant. This concentration in small caps is consistent with limited attention and arbitrage constraints."

**Example for Figure 5:**
> "Figure 5 plots the difference between FOMC and matched non-FOMC days across horizons. All differences are negative and statistically significant, with 95% confidence intervals excluding zero at every horizon. The largest differences occur at short horizons (1-3 days), consistent with immediate attention effects, though the pattern persists through 10 days."

## 🎨 FIGURE STYLE NOTES

- Use Times New Roman font
- Clear axis labels
- Include significance stars (*, **, ***)
- Error bars / confidence intervals where appropriate
- Legend clearly labeled
- High resolution (300 DPI)
- Grayscale-friendly (use patterns, not just colors)
